# 이번 생성 대상: ship

Create ONE new 3D asset for the concept: SHIP (a small sailing ship, used as the icon for the
site's floating Contact launcher — a playful "come join my crew" invitation, referencing a
famous pirate-crew recruitment line from a shonen anime, without using any character, logo, or
copyrighted artwork from that work).

Use the attached Profile asset ONLY as the visual Style Master.
Do not copy its subject or its flat identity-card composition.

This asset belongs to the same "Soft Spatial 3D — v1" icon family.

CONTEXT:
This icon sits alone inside a circular accent-colored glass button that floats above the page
(no card, no badge text next to it) and also appears large in the Contact modal's success
screen. Unlike the other family members, an earlier placeholder version of this asset used a
realistic brown-wood-and-red-sail reference image that looked out of place — too dark and too
"real-world material" next to the rest of the pale-blue-glass/ivory family. This regeneration
replaces that placeholder entirely.

SUBJECT:
A small, simplified toy sailboat, front-facing (not a 3/4 side view), matching the composition of
the attached SHAPE REFERENCE image closely. A previous generation attempt at this same subject
read too abstract — like a cluster of triangles/flags — because the hull was too thin relative to
the sails, and a small flag at the mast top added a third competing triangle shape. This
regeneration must fix both problems: **the hull must be the first thing that reads as "boat,"
before the sails even register, and there must be no flag at all.**

FORM (follow the SHAPE REFERENCE image's proportions closely):
- HULL FIRST: a wide, chunky, clearly boat-shaped hull — a deep rounded scoop/bowl shape, wider
  than it is tall, with a raised rounded rim along the top edge (like the reference image's hull,
  which has a visible thick rim and 2-3 soft horizontal "plank line" grooves — recreate that same
  rim + subtle groove detail, just in glass material instead of wood). The bottom edge of the hull
  must be ONE single smooth, clean curve — no scalloping, no waviness, no bumps along the bottom
  outline. Any texture (the plank-line grooves) stays as thin internal lines well inside the
  silhouette; it must never touch or disturb the outer bottom edge itself. The hull should occupy
  a large, unmistakable portion of the overall silhouette — at least as much visual weight as the
  sails, not a thin sliver underneath them.
- a single centered mast rising from the middle of the hull, slightly tapered
- TWO sails, one on each side of the mast (front-facing, symmetric-ish), each shaped like an
  actual draped cloth sail — a soft curved/bulging outer edge (not a perfectly straight triangle
  edge), tapering to a point at the top near the mast. They should look like billowing fabric,
  not geometric flags.
- MAST HEIGHT: an earlier version left a noticeable bare stretch of mast poking out above the
  sails' peak, which read as an ambiguous, unfinished-looking detail. Fix this — the sails' top
  point must reach all the way up to (or come within a hair of) the very tip of the mast, so the
  mast is fully "wrapped" by the sails along its height and no bare mast segment is visible
  sticking out above them.
- NO flag or pennant at the top of the mast — an earlier version had a small triangular flag
  there and it made the silhouette read as "a cluster of triangles" instead of "a boat." Keep the
  mast top plain/bare except for the sails reaching it (see MAST HEIGHT above) — nothing else
  attached to the mast tip. Simpler is better here.
- overall silhouette priority when viewed small: hull shape reads first, then mast+sails

MATERIAL (v1.1 — color glass is the dominant material, ivory is a minor accent only):
- the hull: translucent color (blue) glass — this is the main surface of the ship and should
  cover most of its visible area, soft and slightly frosted like the rest of the family's glass
  parts
- the sail: translucent color (blue) glass as well, a shade that reads clearly distinct from the
  hull (e.g. a slightly lighter/more frosted blue than the hull, or vice versa) so hull and sail
  remain visually separable
- warm ivory soft plastic: use ONLY as a small accent — e.g. the mast, or a thin trim line along
  the hull's rim — never as the dominant surface of the hull or sail
- smooth rounded geometry, polished but not metallic
- soft specular highlights, same material language as the Style Master
- do NOT use brown wood tones, dark red, or any saturated "real boat" colors — this must read as
  the same color-glass family as every other v1.1 asset, not a literal wooden ship
- do NOT make the hull or sail mostly ivory/white — that reads as nearly invisible on a
  light-mode (near-white) page background, which is the exact problem this regeneration fixes
